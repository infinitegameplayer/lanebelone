A couple of weeks ago I redesigned a [website](https://www.infinitegameos.io/) in about half an hour.

The site was built for "machines" first, for AI answer engines and agents to find and cite. Then humans started showing up. The follow-through wasn't great. People weren't engaging much.

My hypothesis: the site wasn't specifically designed for humans. Now that human eyes were on it, let's beautify it.

I brainstormed what I wanted it to feel like. The depth of what it meant to me, the symbolism. Then, boom. Half an hour later, AI had taken the vision and begun to build it.

Whether it keeps people exploring on the site is an experiment the next couple of months will answer.

A website like that used to cost hundreds of dollars for something cheap or thousands for something good. Even the concept for a refresh took someone days.

That's the side of it where people feel uneasy. I'd feel uneasy too if that were my work. My role. My "job".

Someone at one of my workshops said it out loud: companies won't keep hiring whole teams of comms managers at eighty to a hundred thousand a year. Owners I work with ask the other side of it. Where does AI actually fit in my business? Where do I even start?

For a long time we've been defined by our jobs, our titles and our roles. It's one of the biggest "games" most of us were born into. You fill a role and the role pays you. The money keeps a roof over your head and food in your belly.

The business world feels the pull to categorize and define roles so it can measure whether each one is holding up. It's given a lot of people steady footing.

Livelihood is the financial economics of your life, close to the foundation of the hierarchy of needs. Loosening a role can bring up fear and hesitation.

Someone can play a role for years, even decades, quietly asking, "Is this actually me?" They might be really good at it. Then the big one: it makes the money.

I recently watched Rick Rubin on The Diary of a CEO podcast. Asked about the near-term incentives behind his work, he said, "Never once. Never a consideration." Earlier in the same conversation: "Any label that you put on yourself or that's put on you makes you smaller."

He's talking about labels. I hear roles in there too.

People ask me this often: "What do you do?"

I do AI work with businesses. I take people to Peru. I sell digital products and have run workshops.

I don't look at it as categories.

About four years ago I noticed I'd been trying to fit all of myself through one business. I'm grateful I had the capacity to do so much there.

So I decided I'm always going to be myself. I'd find outlets of expression that align with me.

Keyword: do. The doing is a byproduct of who I am and my natural inclinations. I didn't want to be defined only as an advisor, only as a retreat leader or only as a digital products creator.

That's been a blessing and a challenge. It took me a while to gain momentum, because I chose to build across the whole ecosystem at once. Most people will probably want to go all in on one domain, get a foundation and then move to the next. That's totally fine. It wasn't the game I wanted to play.

There are economic considerations, 100%.

Now the board itself is shifting. The [technologies](https://www.infinitegameos.io/concepts/technology-is-nature-organized) the economy runs on are changing. The roles are rearranging around them. The [rules themselves are in play](https://www.infinitegameos.io/concepts/the-rules-are-in-play). Whether this wave arrives as fast as we think or takes years longer, it's moving fast.

Small business owners, solopreneurs and creators can now have a digital team in addition to a human team, [one that works in service of their vision](https://www.infinitegameos.io/concepts/ai-is-the-apprentice-you-are-the-artist).

I feel like I'm nimbly traversing these emerging technologies, with a lot of uncertainty and a lot of "How will this evolve?" I'm choosing to move with the nature of technology and let myself be woven into the next iteration of the economy.

What would be the coolest thing for me to express or build? Who do I want to be? What do I want to create? What wants to create through me?

This is an opportunity. We all get to participate and choose.

Maybe the work ahead becomes a chosen way to spend our time with others, because it feels Alive. Exciting. Authentic. Creative energy moving through us, yielding value into the world, with financial reciprocity as the natural byproduct.

So, what do you do?

Who would you be if money weren't tied to a role you "had" to do? What would you build?

I don't know exactly where my own answer leads. But I'm exploring it each week.
