A lot of the people I've talked with lately have several irons in the fire.

A project here. An offer there. A potential collaboration that just landed in the inbox. When I ask how the options look, the answer comes back as some version of the same thing. Any one of them is awesome. All of them are pretty amazing (usually).

That's a treat. Several amazing options is a really great "problem" to have.

Then the mind steps in. *Okay, yeah, but I'm going to miss out on one of them.*

I know that voice well, from my own head. Underneath it sits a question a lot of builders carry into their week.

How do I stop missing opportunities?

The usual answer is to hold every door open. Track everything. Say yes before the window closes. Keep a quiet list of the ones that got away (you know what I'm talking about).

When people tell me this, I ask them something back. When you ask yourself which option feels most alive, who's answering?

There are two places an answer can usually come from.

One is the "character". The human you, with a name and a calendar and a bank balance. The character answers from the mind. It reasons in "if this, then that." It's often trying to justify something or solve a problem. Sometimes it sounds like fear: *I need to do this.* It wants to know exactly what's on the other side before it says yes.

The other is the "player". The [Infinite Player](https://www.sidequesthq.co/infinite-player) is the deeper part of you, the one playing the whole game across every round of it. When the player answers, the energy spreads through the body. It feels good. It can feel exciting or peaceful or like a deep knowing. Sometimes it's a whisper. *Go check that out.*

The tell is in the language. Do I feel like I *need* to do it? Or do I get to choose excitement here? Do I get to choose aliveness?

Finite logic sees a game with an ending, so every missed opening looks like points lost for good. It feels contracted, a list of options that keeps narrowing. Infinite logic knows the game keeps going, so an opening that passes is one moment in a much longer play. It feels spacious.

To the Infinite Player, there's no problem to solve. There are [beautiful puzzles to explore](https://www.infinitegameos.io/concepts/puzzles-not-problems).

That's why the player carries no regret in missing out. There's always another opportunity, because [the game is infinite](https://www.infinitegameos.io/infinite-game).

The character has its own wisdom too.

From the character's seat, everything is finite. A conversation ends. A hike ends. A project (or quest) has a beginning, middle and end. So does a season of a business. That ending is what lets the character bring a fine focal point of presence to whatever's in front of it. It's what makes a finite life so juicy and richly textured.

So choices made from the character's view are part of the fun. The detours are part of the journey. Where we can get lost is when the character is the only one steering.

The play is a communion between the two. The player setting the destinations (GPS). The character at the steering wheel, bringing the focus.

In practice it's simple. I'm still refining my craft with it. When an opportunity lands, big or small, I assume the Infinite Player and answer nonchalantly, playfully. Does this feel good? Does it feel exciting? Am I hem-hawing? Or am I pumped about it?

Not everything has to be super exciting. Peaceful is part of the flavor. So is soothing, nourishing, electric. The question is whether it feels appropriate for where I'm at today.

In that light state, the truth almost always shows up vividly clear. And the answers come in more shapes than yes or no.

Do it.

Don't do it.

Yes, but do it a little differently.

Don't do it, but take note.

Those small textures are where you get to know yourself. Each one refines your own internal operating system for how you engage with projects, collaborators, work and play.

I'm about to head somewhere soon without a "concrete" reason. There's something intriguing there, a tiny mystery. I'm curious about it. The character would love to know what's on the other side first. The player already said yes. There's an edge of the unknown in it, a healthy thrill, a texture of beautiful anticipation.

So if there are three good opportunities sitting in your inbox this week, gratitude is one way to hold them. Whichever you choose, you really can't lose in a certain kind of way.

Perhaps all of them can be answered. If only one or two fit, the player can choose. Which one feels the most appropriate, the most exciting, the most Alive?

Then trust that it'll all work out.

There have been so many times I thought an opportunity had slipped away. The timing was off. Or it landed on top of something else. Weeks later it came back around as a different opportunity, even better than the one I thought I'd lost.
