When I got out of the army, the uniform came off.

The jacket stayed on.

In Special Forces we planned for everything. What could go wrong, what could go right and backup plans to backup plans to backup plans. Primary, Alternate, Contingency and Emergency. Military people know it as PACE. All of it gives the mission the greatest chance of success.

Then I was living down in Panama, trying several different things: experiments, projects, ideas I wanted to test. I brought the planning with me into every one of them, mapping out the beginning, middle and end of something before I moved forward.

What kept showing up was a sense of rigidity, a lack of fluidity. Things that didn't go according to "plan". Me, asking, "Why isn't it working?"

People in transition ask me some version of the same question. It can come after a company lets its people go and someone decides to work for themselves. Or from someone who has accomplished so many things in their life:

"What am I even good at anymore?"

After years of being good at something, it's a question that can arise when moving into something new. It often comes from a state of doubt. It can also come from a state of transition.

It may be a "jacket" coming off.

Identities are jackets. Roles, archetypes and attributes we put on, learn to wear well and eventually take off. You can welcome the energy of one without declaring it as [who you are](https://www.infinitegameos.io/concepts/conditioned-vs-authentic-identity). The jackets are not you.

Being very good at something can lead to you identifying as it. Do it for many years, over and over. It becomes "unconscious competence". Automatic. Invisible.

That's how I applied military precision planning to lifestyle design, to entrepreneurship, to life after the army. I didn't even know I was doing it.

Life has a way of pointing it out. Sometimes through circumstance. Sometimes a situation. Sometimes an internal knowing that it's time to move in a new direction. It can feel like friction, challenge or a disappointing circumstance.

At the time, the friction didn't feel like the gift. Now I see it was the gift wrapped up in the friction.

Life was ushering me into a richer experience. Less led by the head. More open to the intelligence of feeling, rather than just mental "strategery." Amazing experiences that weren't part of the plan.

So I welcomed a new jacket. Playful flow.

People still come to me for strategy, for structure, for planning. I still have significant structure in my life. Precision, architecture and systematic thinking, brought forth when advantageous.

The structure didn't change. My grip on it did.

Now [the structure holds me](https://www.infinitegameos.io/concepts/held-by-structure), so I can dance with the aliveness moving through me. When I get out on the trail, that jacket doesn't come with me. When I'm playing D&D, it mostly sits it out. (A little strategy sneaks in. It's D&D.)

It's like trying on jackets in a store. You put one on, move around, look in the mirror. "How does that look?" There's no obligation to buy the jacket.

Then one feels good. "Yeah, that looks pretty solid. That looks fun."

When Tim Ferriss started his podcast, he committed to six episodes. He expected it might quietly end there. He tried on the jacket of podcaster. He's worn it often since.

I run my experiments in a similar way. A (side) quest with a beginning, a middle and an end. A hypothesis. Usually short-term.

The same beginning, middle and end that once held me rigid now holds the experiment. Choosing a quest, running it and closing it is what the [Side Quest Playbook](https://www.sidequesthq.co/products/side-quest-playbook) is built around.

It brings a playful nonchalance. [Experimentation for its own sake](https://www.infinitegameos.io/concepts/playgrounds-of-exploration). A loosening of importance. You don't have to make it who you are. Who you are remains, whether the jacket is on or off.

Sometimes life puts us in situations to help us realize what the next jacket to experiment with is. When one gets taken off you, it can look like a setback. It can also look like life pointing at the next jacket to try. You get to choose which one you're holding.

Try on the next jacket. The next project. The next thing. Give it enough structure that you don't feel lost.

Run the experiment. See how you feel at the end. Let the feeling tell you whether to keep going, try a new experiment or go all in.
