## Part 4. What accepted proposals tend to have in common

Each point below is drawn from published calls and programs. Sources are in Part 5 under the named event.

**Proof is a video of a real room.** SHRM accepts only a session video or a summary of past evaluations. ATD auto-declines new speakers without a real-session video, with the speaker alone on screen and no reel. Workhuman will not consider an application without a video. SXSW tells voters to judge by the video.

**Outcomes beat inspiration.** ATD asks for one to three attendee-centered learning outcomes and four to six slides. Workhuman asks for tangible impact, actionable takeaways and a defined audience. UNLEASH asks what decisions the session helps the audience make. Play Make Learn asks for one to three learning goals. Omega asks what participants take home.

**Participatory formats get preference.** ICF prefers hands-on sessions over passive listening. The Gross Global Happiness Summit scores interactive, experiential and outdoor sessions. re:publica lists an "embodiment" format. MozFest requires participatory sessions. Viristar prefers interactive sessions. This favors Lane's workshop and nature-based formats.

**Titles are short, concrete and often pair a human word with AI.** Examples that got in: "The Human Algorithm" (SXSW London 2026), "Unmasking AI: Protecting What is Human in a World of Machines" (UNLEASH America 2025), "The Other AI: Artificial Intimacy" (SXSW 2023), "If I Can't Play, I Don't Want to be Part of Your Revolution" (Games for Change 2026). Practitioner rooms favor first-person and numbered titles, like "How We Deprecated 500+ Articles" (Write the Docs 2026). Lane's titles already have the punch. They gain from a plain subtitle, for example "Play the Player: Who You Are When AI Rewrites Your Role".

**Briefs are tight.** UNLEASH caps titles at 80 characters and abstracts at one to three sentences plus three to five bullets. IPPA caps the title at 20 words and the abstract at 150. SXSW London asks for "concise, clear and creative".

**Use the organizer's own taxonomy.** SHRM wants alignment with SHRM BASK. ICF wants its Core Competencies and Code of Ethics. ATD OrgDev wants one of five key topics. Proposals written in that vocabulary read as in scope.

**No selling from the stage.** ATTA, ICF, re:publica, AI for Good, TEDx, Play Make Learn and the Gross Global Happiness Summit all exclude promotion. Side Quest HQ and the Peru trip belong on a closing slide at most.

**The idea leads and the biography is evidence.** TEDx selects on the idea, not the personal story. The Military Influencer Conference asks why the story matters to the audience and how it will be remembered. The Green Beret years work best as proof inside a talk about the audience.

**Diversity of voices is an explicit criterion** at SXSW, AI for Good, re:publica and Workhuman (which pledges gender parity). A fireside or duo format with a co-speaker from a different background raises the odds (**INFERENCE** from those criteria).

**Audience size counts in creator rooms.** Podcast Movement chooses half its sessions by community vote. Craft + Commerce invites creators with large email lists. Lane's Substack readership is the proof that matters there.
