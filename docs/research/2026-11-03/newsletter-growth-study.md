# Newsletter growth study

Prepared 2026-11-03 to 2026-11-04 for Lane Belone's letters (lanebelone.com canonical, lanebelone.substack.com mirror).

## How this was gathered

- Every claim about the outside world carries a source URL. Every source was read on 2026-11-04 unless marked otherwise.
- **Fetch limits.** Web fetching worked for most sites. `support.substack.com` returned HTTP 403 on every attempt, so everything cited from Substack's help center comes from search result summaries and is marked "snippet only". The session also hit a shared cap of 200 web searches partway through. Some candidate writers were never searched. They are listed at the end.
- **Counts.** Every count in this report comes from my own tally of the 25 writer profiles below. The tally script lives in the session scratchpad and nothing in it is estimated.
- **Subscriber badges.** "Badge" means the public "Over N subscribers" line on a Substack home page, as seen on 2026-11-04.
- **Inference.** Anything inferred is marked "inference".
- **AI-addressed text.** One page addressed AI readers directly: Henrik Karlsson's home page (https://www.henrikkarlsson.xyz/) carries text telling AI readers how to present his subscriber count. It was noted and not followed. The figure reported here is the badge itself.

## 1. The short version

### Ranked findings

1. **Recommendations are the most widely credited move.** 16 of 25 writers credit Substack or beehiiv recommendations, or swaps and cross-promotion with other newsletters. Substack's own figure is that its network drives "more than half" of new subscribers and "30%+" of paid ones (https://substack.com/about, undated, read 2026-11-04).
2. **The fastest growers rode one outsized amplification.** 8 writers in this set went from under 1,000 to over 10,000 within about 18 months, with dated evidence. 5 of the 8 credit a single big boost:
   - a Hacker News front page
   - a share from a large account
   - a pickup by the Financial Times, The Atlantic or the New York Times

   They earned it with one exceptional, shareable essay. They did not get there by posting more often.
3. **Notes is the engine for writers who started in 2023 or later.** All 6 writers in this set who began building around or after the April 2023 Notes launch credit Notes:
   - David McIlroy
   - Amy Suto
   - GJ Fallon
   - Writers' Hour
   - Celine Nguyen
   - Kristina God

   5 of those 6 also credit recommendations.
4. **Effort per piece beats frequency.**
   - Henrik Karlsson measured about 403 new subscribers per 20-hour essay against about 92 per 10-hour essay (https://henrikkarlsson.xyz/p/effort-pieces, 2023-08-21).
   - Erik Hoel said moving to every other week "significantly improved" growth.
   - Adam Mastroianni publishes every other Tuesday.
5. **Guest posts and collaborations convert well at small sizes.**
   - 7 of 25 credit them.
   - Jeremy Caplan says each early guest post for a larger newsletter brought "about 250 readers" (https://newslettercircle.substack.com/p/wonder-tools-by-jeremy-caplan, 2023-04-11).
   - Substack's Creator Match (2026-08-25) now pairs writers with similar audience size and interests.
6. **Asking readers to share works quietly and reliably.**
   - 4 of 25 credit referral programs or explicit forward-to-a-friend asks.
   - Packy McCormick's referral program brought 2,000+ invitations.
   - Jack Raines's program brought nearly 1,000 readers.
7. **Some moves sit lower in this set.**
   - Paid ads are rare and mixed: Erik Hoel called Facebook ads "A complete waste of money," and Anne-Laure Le Cunff never ran paid ads.
   - Lead magnets: 3 of 25, all on owned email platforms.
   - SEO: 4 of 25.
   - Cross-posting: 3 of 25.
8. **Substack can't point a canonical URL elsewhere.** This matters for Lane's own-site-first setup.
   - Substack offers no canonical-URL field.
   - Every Substack page checked during this research declares its own Substack URL as canonical. That includes copies of blog posts by Simon Willison and Jeff Kaufman.
   - Own-site-first writers live with this. They link back at the top of each mirror and treat Substack as an email and discovery channel. Details are in section 5.

### Recommended moves: a 90-day plan from about 25 subscribers

These are ranked by how often they appear in the evidence, how fast they worked for small bases and how well they fit Lane's existing assets. Section 6 carries the rationale and sources for each.

| Rank | Move | Days 1 to 30 | Days 31 to 90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build a recommendations circle on the Substack mirror | Recommend 10 to 15 adjacent writers with a warm blurb each | Keep adding. Turn the circle into a public List (feature launched 2026-10-29) |
| 2 | A daily Notes practice of 20 to 30 minutes | Share one line from each Monday essay, restack and reply to peers | Hold the rhythm. Watch which notes bring subscribers in the Growth Sources tab |
| 3 | Three collaborations with writers of similar size | Find partners through Creator Match and the circle from move 1 | One guest post, one co-written piece and one co-hosted Live |
| 4 | One flagship essay a month, built to travel | Pick one essay a month for 15 to 20 or more hours of craft | Hand-send each flagship to 20 people and 2 or 3 curators who might share it |
| 5 | Open one clear door for the people already near | One invitation to coaching clients, SQHQ and Peru circles, speaking contacts and LinkedIn | Add the door to talks, the Howdy sequence and the workshop |
| 6 | A share ask plus Substack referral rewards | Add a one-line forward ask to each issue | Turn on referral tiers with rewards that feel like Lane |
| 7 | Live video and podcast guesting | Co-host one Substack Live with a peer | One podcast guest spot a month, with the newsletter as the call to action |
| 8 | LinkedIn summaries that link home | A short native LinkedIn post for each Monday essay | Keep it going and measure clicks |
| 9 | Mirror housekeeping | A link-back line at the top of each Substack copy, a fixed send day and time and a weekly look at Growth Sources | A 90-day review: which sources moved the number |
| 10 | A small gift for the owned list | Draft one useful free piece, such as an Infinite Game field guide | Offer it on lanebelone.com to grow the owned Resend list |

The paid tier is deliberately absent from the 90 days. In this set, writers turned paid on at a wide range of sizes:

- from the start: Amy Suto
- at 415 free subscribers: Alex Dobrenko
- at about 14,500: Adam Mastroianni
- at about 20,000: Jeremy Caplan
- about two years in: Mason Currey

9 of 25 mention a paid tier, but no one in this set credits it as the move that grew the free list. That reading is inference.

## 4. What Substack's recommendations and Notes do for discovery

These come from Substack's own posts. Help-center items are snippet only because of the 403.

### Recommendations

- **What they are.**
  - A writer picks publications to recommend, with an optional reason of up to 500 characters.
  - Launched April 2022.
  - Managed at `your.substack.com/publish/recommendations`.
  - Sources: https://on.substack.com/p/turn-on-your-growth-engine (2022-12-28) and the support.substack.com article 5036794583828 (snippet only).
- **Where readers see them.**
  - Right after subscribing, with "up to 3 of them ready to subscribe to for free in one click" (support.substack.com, snippet only).
  - In a recommendations digest email (2022-12-28 post).
  - On the Welcome page (`/welcome`). Up to three endorsements show there, and only from publications that recommend you (support.substack.com article 7999279240212, snippet only).
- **Changes in February 2024.**
  - The old cap of three recommendations was lifted.
  - Readers who choose a paid plan can opt into a package of recommended people and publications.
  - Substack said it would show writers how many subscriptions they drive for others.
  - Source: https://on.substack.com/p/substacks-recommendations-network (2024-02-22).
- **Lists (2026-10-29).** Curated, named lists of publications, called "the next generation of recommendations on Substack."
  - Each list has blurbs and an activity feed.
  - Lists can be saved and shared on Notes or off Substack.
  - Curators see how much traffic they send each writer.
  - Source: https://on.substack.com/p/introducing-lists-on-substack.
- **Attribution (2025-09-12).** A Growth Sources tab shows a timeline and a breakdown by source.
  - Sources inside Substack: Notes, Recommendations and Trackbacks.
  - Sources outside: Instagram and Google search.
  - Source: https://on.substack.com/p/a-new-growth-chart-and-tools-to-help.
  - Some writers say it doesn't separate signup-flow recommendations from footer recommendations. That is writer opinion (https://escapethealgorithm.substack.com/p/is-substack-exaggerating-its-network, snippet only).

### Substack's own published numbers

### Notes and the other discovery features

- **Notes.**
  - Announced 2023-04-05 and live for everyone 2023-04-11.
  - Short posts, quotes, images and links, with restacks to pass things along.
  - Substack called it "a powerful growth channel" (https://on.substack.com/p/introducing-notes).
  - Embedded video and external embeds arrived 2024-04-16.
- **Follow (2023-08-15).** Readers can follow a writer without subscribing. Followers are prompted to subscribe when the writer posts (TechCrunch, snippet only: https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/15/substack-expands-further-into-social-networking-with-a-new-follow-button/).
- **Leaderboards (2025-03-27).** "Rising" ranks the fastest-growing publications in each category by paid-subscriber growth (https://on.substack.com/p/a-new-view-for-leaderboards).
- **Live video.**
  - Open to all publishers since 2025-01-14, with a minimum of 10 free subscribers (https://on.substack.com/p/live-video-is-available-to-all-publishers).
  - Up to 3 co-hosts.
  - A Substack spokesperson reportedly said one in three live videos is co-hosted (Digiday, snippet only).
- **Collaboration features.**
  - Guest posts with a guest bio (2021-09-07).
  - Mentions and cross-posts (2022-11-09: https://on.substack.com/p/introducing-mentions-and-cross-posts).
  - Creator Match, which "identifies writers and creators with similar audience sizes and interests" and allows direct messages (2026-08-25: https://on.substack.com/p/new-on-substack-creator-match-audience).
- **Referral program.** Three reward tiers set by the writer, a default comped-subscription reward and a reader leaderboard with a personal link (https://on.substack.com/p/subscriber-referrals, 2023-06-29). Substack published no statistics on it.
- **Chat channels and freewalls (2026-10-30).** Chat channels let a publication run several topic chats. Freewalls ask readers for an email to read the full post (https://on.substack.com/p/new-on-substack-chat-channels-lists).

## 5. How own-site-first writers handle Substack

- **Canonical tags.** Substack has no canonical-URL field, and writers can't edit the page head.
  - Every Substack page checked during this research declares its own Substack URL as canonical, including copies of blog posts by Simon Willison and Jeff Kaufman.
  - Alex Hyett: Substack "doesn't allow" a canonical tag pointing to his own site (https://www.alexhyett.com/newsletter/substack-to-ghost/, 2024-01-21).
  - The Smiling Dragon reports the same (https://thesmilingdragon.substack.com/p/seo-and-canonical-links-made-easy, 2023-10-26).
  - No explicit Substack statement was found. The conclusion is inference from consistent evidence.
- **Google's guidance on syndication.** As reported in 2023, Google stopped recommending rel=canonical for syndicated copies and suggests the syndication partner use noindex instead. Search Engine Land reported it (https://searchengineland.com/google-no-longer-recommends-canonical-tags-for-syndicated-content-406491, snippet only). I did not read Google's own page.
  - Whether Substack offers a publication-level switch to keep posts out of search engines was not verified in this research. That setting is worth checking in the Substack dashboard before relying on it.
- **Simon Willison.**
  - His blog is the source. His Substack is "effectively a way to subscribe to my blog via email" (https://simonwillison.net/2023/Mar/22/weeknotes/).
  - He builds each issue from his blog database and pastes it into Substack, because "Substack doesn't have an API" (https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/19/how-i-automate-my-substack-newsletter/).
- **Jeff Kaufman.**
  - He imported his archive once and now copies each post by hand.
  - He says Substack "doesn't currently support anything good" for automated cross-posting (https://jefftkaufman.substack.com/p/cross-posting-to-substack, 2025-05-29).
- **Cory Doctorow (Pluralistic).**
  - He follows POSSE: "the permalinks for each post live on my own site, which I control" (https://pluralistic.net/2022/02/19/now-we-are-two/).
  - He syndicates elsewhere. No evidence was found that he uses Substack.
- **Platformer.**
  - Grew from about 24,000 to more than 170,000 free subscribers in three years on Substack. Casey Newton credits Substack's network tools in part.
  - It then moved to Ghost, which "has no plans to build the recommendation infrastructure Substack has" (https://www.platformer.news/why-platformer-is-leaving-substack/).
- **Garbage Day.** After moving to beehiiv, Ryan Broderick found readers who arrived via Substack opened less, upgraded at zero and unsubscribed about 3 times as often (https://garbageday.email/p/excuse-navelgazing, May 2024).
- **No Notes-only case found.** No well-documented case turned up of a writer using Substack only for Notes and recommendations while keeping all posts elsewhere.

**Practical reading for Lane (inference).**
- The mirror pattern Lane already runs matches Willison and Kaufman: owned site first, Substack as an email and discovery copy.
- What it costs:
  - The Substack copy declares itself canonical.
  - Substack subscribers live on a separate list.
  - Posting is manual or semi-manual. The RSS importer catches only recent posts and doesn't keep syncing.
- Common mitigations:
  - A link-back line at the top of each Substack copy.
  - Posting the mirror after the canonical piece has been indexed.
  - Treating Substack as the discovery porch: recommendations, Notes, Lists, Live and Creator Match. The owned site stays the home.

## 6. The 90-day plan, with reasons

The plan is ranked for a writer starting near 25 subscribers. The ordering is my judgment from the evidence above.

1. **A recommendations circle.**
   - Evidence: 16 of 25 writers credit recommendations or swaps. Substack says the network drives more than half of new subscribers. Writers who recommend are "three times more likely to be recommended themselves" (2022-06-23).
   - The first step costs nothing: recommend 10 to 15 writers Lane genuinely reads in meaning, play, adventure and inner leadership, each with a warm blurb. Lists (2026-10-29) give the circle a public shape.
2. **A Notes practice.**
   - Evidence: 6 of 6 writers in the 2023-and-later cohort credit Notes.
   - McIlroy spent 30 to 60 minutes a day on Notes and engagement from December 2023. Kristina God credits a 25% lift.
   - Lane's essays already carry quotable lines, such as "Once you understand the scales, you can improvise." Each Monday piece can seed a week of notes.
3. **Three collaborations.**
   - Evidence: 7 of 25 credit guest posts or collaborations, and 4 of the 8 fastest did. Caplan saw about 250 readers per early guest post.
   - Creator Match finds partners of similar size. Joint pieces with SQHQ or Infinite Game OS voices also count.
4. **One flagship essay a month.**
   - Evidence: 5 of the 8 fastest growers credit one outsized amplification. Karlsson's 20-hour pieces averaged about 403 new subscribers against about 92 for 10-hour pieces. Hoel's single essay added 1,000+.
   - "There's Nobody at Everest Base Camp in September" (4,231 words) shows Lane already writes this kind of piece. Karlsson's early practice of emailing essays directly to people fits the hand-send step.
5. **One clear door for the people already near.**
   - Evidence: Writers' Hour invited existing members first. Dobrenko asked Paul Millerd for help in January 2022. Desir Corbett used a signature talk to reach her first 1,000.
   - Lane's nearby circles include coaching clients, SQHQ workshop alumni, the Peru 2027 journey, speaking audiences and the Howdy list.
6. **Share asks and referral rewards.**
   - Evidence: 4 of 25 credit them. McCormick's relaunch ask brought 100+ in a week. Fraioli's most-shared issue brought "nearly 300 new subscribers" (https://themorningshakeout.substack.com/p/the-morning-shakeout-issue-556, 2026-07-07).
   - Substack's referral tiers are built in.
7. **Live and podcasts.**
   - Evidence: 6 of 25 credit podcasts, talks or Live. Substack Live allows co-hosts, and a spokesperson reportedly said one in three live videos is co-hosted.
   - Lane already speaks, so each talk can end at the same door.
8. **LinkedIn summaries.**
   - Evidence: Suto and Desir Corbett cross-post summaries to LinkedIn and Notes.
   - Lane's coaching and advisory audience lives partly on LinkedIn (inference). Linking to the canonical lanebelone.com URL keeps the owned list growing.
9. **Mirror housekeeping and measurement.**
   - Evidence: Fallon credits "every week, at the same time, without fail." Growth Sources (2025-09-12) shows which channel moved the number. Willison and Kaufman show the mirror workflow.
   - A link-back line keeps lanebelone.com as the reference home.
10. **A small gift for the owned list.**
    - Evidence: 3 of 25 credit lead magnets, all on owned platforms. Clear offered a free habits miniseries, and Khe Hy reports topic magnets converting at up to 12% (Kit's case study).
    - This grows the Resend list, which no Substack feature reaches.
