# The Infinite Player: comparable titles and agents scan

Research date: 2026-11-03 session, sources read 2026-11-04 unless marked otherwise.
Prepared for Lane Belone. The book is *The Infinite Player*, trade nonfiction in progress.

## Ranked findings

1. **The shelf has an open seat.** Apart from Simon Sinek's business book, I found no recent US trade book that treats the Infinite Game as a way of living. The closest is Niki Harré's *The Infinite Game: How to Live Well Together* (Auckland University Press, 2018), which is a New Zealand university press title now out of print in paperback. (Inference from the 40-title scan below.)

2. **The single closest comp is C. Thi Nguyen's *The Score: How to Stop Playing Somebody Else's Game* (Penguin Press, January 2026).** It argues that scores and metrics quietly rewrite what we value and invites readers to choose their own game. That is the finite-game critique The Infinite Player answers with a way of playing. Source: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/735252/the-score-by-c-thi-nguyen/

3. **The newsletter-to-deal path is now routine and documented.** Publishers Weekly deal notices from 2025 and 2026 name a Substack or podcast as the platform for at least five comparable sales: Jeannine Ouellette (Rodale), Julie Fratantoni (Little, Brown Spark), Vanessa Cornell (Dial), Victoria Hutchins (Convergent) and Michael Lange (Bloomsbury). None of those notices give subscriber counts. Sources in the imprints and platform sections.

4. **Small lists have sold.** Mikala Jamison was approached by an agent "in April 2023, when I had fewer than 2,000 subscribers" and later got a book deal. Paul Millerd had "about 3,000 subscribers" when he self-published *The Pathless Path*. Sources: https://on.substack.com/p/grow-how-mikala-jamison-turned-her and https://pathlesspath.com/200k-publishing-offer/

5. **The strongest agent fit is Laurie Abkemeier at DeFiore and Company.** Her own agency page lists Cal Newport (*Slow Productivity*, *Digital Minimalism*, *Deep Work*), Brad Stulberg (*The Practice of Groundedness*, *Master of Change*) and Steve Magness (*Win the Inside Game*). She takes email queries only and says response time "is generally no more than two weeks." Source: https://www.defliterary.com/agents/laurie-abkemeier (read 2026-11-04, confirmed by direct fetch).

6. **Penguin Random House imprints dominate this shelf.** By my tally of the table below, 19 of the 40 comps came from PRH imprints. Portfolio has 5 (Sinek, A. Brooks, Godin, Newport, Mollick). Penguin Press has 4 (Nguyen, Keltner, Rubin, Haidt). Random House and Riverhead have 2 each. Avery, Dial, Crown, Doubleday, Viking and Ballantine have 1 each.

7. **Agents are rarely named in public.** Only 10 of the 40 comps carry an agent or agency name from a public source (my tally). The rest need acknowledgments pages or Publishers Marketplace.

8. **Title collision to plan for.** Simon Sinek's *The Infinite Game* (Portfolio, 2019) and Harré's book of the same name both sit in the same search results as "Infinite Player." The proposal will read stronger if it names Sinek once, as the business application of Carse, and then shows the life application. (Inference.)

## Recommended moves

1. **Lead the query with *The Score* and *Tiny Experiments*.** One is the philosophy of choosing your own game. The other is the newsletter author who made life into a playground of experiments. Together they place The Infinite Player at the meeting point of both.
2. **Query Laurie Abkemeier first, then a tight first wave of five.** Suggested first wave: Abkemeier (DeFiore), Caroline Eisenmann (Frances Goldin), Jane von Mehren (Aevitas), Laura Mazer (Wendy Sherman) and Steve Harris (CSG). Each has a public query path and a recent comparable deal. (Inference from the agent table.)
3. **Write the full proposal before querying.** Lucinda Literary marks the proposal as required. Levine Greenberg Rostan asks for comps, audience and marketing. DiMona asks for a proposal plus "why this book, why you and why now." A ready proposal covers every page in the agent table.
4. **Publish the subscriber count and growth rate, not only the newsletter's existence.** Deal notices name the newsletter. Agents ask for "Platform & Network" (Lucinda Literary). A dated count and a monthly growth figure answer that field directly. (Inference.)
5. **Feature one essay as the seed.** Jenny Odell's *How to Do Nothing* grew from one talk transcript on Medium. "The Infinite Game Is Already in Progress" or "Nobody's Good at It Yet" could play that role in the proposal. (Inference.)
6. **Use the Green Beret story as the hook and the Infinite Game as the idea.** Comparable authors who sold on credentials (Varol, ex-rocket scientist) still led with one big idea. (Inference.)
7. **Respect the AI-written work bans.** Frances Goldin, The Gernert Company and Wendy Sherman Associates state they decline AI-written submissions. The query and proposal need to be Lane's own prose.

## The five strongest comps for the query letter

1. ***The Score* by C. Thi Nguyen (Penguin Press, 2026).** The closest idea on the shelf: it diagnoses the borrowed finite game, and The Infinite Player offers the way of playing that comes after it.
2. ***Tiny Experiments* by Anne-Laure Le Cunff (Avery, 2025).** A newsletter writer's book on living freely through playful experiments, which mirrors Lane's platform and his Side Quest idea of finite arcs inside an infinite life.
3. ***Meditations for Mortals* by Oliver Burkeman (FSG, 2024).** The examined-life reader who loved Burkeman's finitude is the reader ready for an infinite orientation, and the book proves a short, warm, practice-led format sells.
4. ***The Power of Fun* by Catherine Price (Dial Press, 2021).** It makes play and aliveness the subject and pairs it with stepping back from screens, the same pairing as Lane's living-beside-AI thread.
5. ***Enchantment* by Katherine May (Riverhead, 2023).** A Substack author's warm, first-person book on wonder that went to a 13-way UK auction, which shows the commercial appetite for the register Lane writes in.

Kept off the list on purpose: Sinek (title collision and a megaseller) and Rubin (celebrity platform). Agents often read megaseller comps as unrealistic. (Inference, general industry practice, not sourced here.)

## How comparable authors built platform before the deal

Five patterns, each with sources (read 2026-11-04).

**1. A newsletter or creator business grows, then the deal follows.** This is the path closest to Lane's.

- Anne-Laure Le Cunff launched Ness Labs in summer 2019 and reached about 3,000 subscribers within two months and about 8,000 by 2020 (https://kit.com/resources/creator-stories/anne-laure-le-cunff). A Product Hunt AMA around 2021 reports 35,000 subscribers and 2,500 paid community members (https://www.producthunt.com/discussions/growing-a-newsletter-and-a-website-to-35k-subscribers-and-100k-visitors-per-month-in-2-years-ama). The count at signing was not found.
- Sahil Bloom's 2022 deal notice cites his newsletter *The Curiosity Chronicle* with no count (PW, Oct 3, 2022, URL above). His site now says "over 800,000 subscribers," undated (https://www.sahilbloom.com/about).

**2. Small, engaged lists can be enough.**

- Mikala Jamison: "In April 2023, when I had fewer than 2,000 subscribers," an agent at Ayesha Pande Literary emailed her. She later got a book deal (https://on.substack.com/p/grow-how-mikala-jamison-turned-her).

**3. Self-publish, prove sales, then publishers come.**

- Paul Millerd self-published *The Pathless Path* in January 2022 with "about 3,000 subscribers" (https://pathlesspath.com/200k-publishing-offer/). He reports about $7,000 in production costs, about 2,000 copies a month at the time of the Penguin offer and over $300k in royalties (https://pathlesspath.com/about/).

A December 2025 interview gives $70k plus $130k (https://natashatynes.substack.com/p/i-turned-down-penguin-a-conversation). I found no Hachette deal for Millerd.
- Jim Murphy's *Inner Excellence* went from self-published to a three-book Grand Central auction (PW, March 10, 2025, URL above).

**4. One talk or essay becomes the book.**

- Jenny Odell's 2017 EYEO keynote, posted as a Medium transcript, became *How to Do Nothing* (https://medium.com/@the_jennitaur/how-to-do-nothing-57e100f59bbb). That book's success drove a 12-bidder US auction for *Saving Time* (FGLA rights sheet, URL above).
- Simon Sinek's 2010 TED talk preceded *The Infinite Game* by nine years (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Sinek).

**5. A column or credential carries one big idea.**

- Oliver Burkeman wrote the Guardian column "This Column Will Change Your Life" from 2006 to 2020 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Burkeman).
- Arthur Brooks writes The Atlantic's "How to Build a Life." Cal Newport has kept the Study Hacks blog since 2007. Keltner, Nguyen, Setiya, Vallor and Summerfield all sold from university posts (sources in the comps table).

What this means for Lane (inference): his assets map onto patterns 1, 3 and 5. He has a Substack, a co-authored 2020 book from a small press and a Green Beret story as a credential. A dated subscriber count, one featured essay and a clear sales history for *Unleash Your Humble Alpha* would answer the platform question agents ask.
